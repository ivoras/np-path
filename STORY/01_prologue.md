# Chapter 01 — PROLOGUE

> **Source:** PATH.pdf pp. 2–7
> **Runtime:** 18–22 min
> **Voices:** Traveler (primary) → Watcher → Traveler → Watcher
> **Cuts:** 4
> **Puzzle:** The Great Lens (optical alignment)
> **Coda:** *"Failure. / The loop. / Static."*

---

## 1. Thematic Function

This chapter has one job: **establish that there are two of you, and that neither can move.**

Everything here is stasis. The Traveler arrives and stops. The Watcher climbs and sits. Twilight
rides the path and *neither of us moved.* The player must leave this chapter with a physical,
bodily sense of two locked bodies looking at each other across an impossible distance — and with
the suspicion, not yet confirmed, that the distance is zero.

Do not explain the Cut. Do not explain the Watcher. Do not name anyone. The player should finish
Chapter 1 unsure whether they played one character or two, and should care about the answer.

---

## 2. Spatial Flow

The chapter is a **rain corridor** that opens into a **ruin**, then inverts into a **tower interior**.
Rough beat map:

| # | Space | Verb | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Wet Road** — a bone-white track through flooded moorland, ember rim-light on both verges | Walk forward. Nothing else is possible. | 3 min |
| 2 | **The Broken Fence** — first man-made object; a gate hanging by one hinge | Push through | 1 min |
| 3 | **The Orchard** — dead fruit trees in ranks, black bark, fruit rotted into the mud, ankle-deep water | Wander; the ranks funnel you | 4 min |
| 4 | **The House** — roofless, one wall collapsed, ivy in the ribs of it | Approach | 3 min |
| 5 | **The Figure** — standing in the ruin, facing you, motionless | Stop. The game takes control of nothing; it simply gives you nothing else to do. | 2 min |
| — | **CUT** | — | — |
| 6 | **The Lower Mansion** — interior, dark, a door being knocked on | Do not open it. Climb. | 3 min |
| 7 | **The Turret Stair** — wooden, squealing, spiralling | Climb | 1 min |
| 8 | **The Turret** — the lens, the cup, the wind | **PUZZLE** | 5 min |

### Level-design rules for this chapter

- **The path carries loose grit and scattered stones.** Not decoration: a bare ribbon under a
  distant horizon gives the eye almost no optic flow, and walking on it reads as standing still.
  Surface detail streaming under the feet, and dark stones sweeping past close to the camera, are
  what make forward motion legible. Every chapter's path gets them.
- **The player can never step off the path in beat 1.** Not with an invisible wall — with water.
  The moor either side is flooded to a depth that reads as *drowning*, and the character will not
  enter it. This teaches the game's only rule: the path is the world.
- **The orchard is the first place you are allowed to stray**, and straying is rewarded with
  nothing except more orchard. Reward curiosity with silence. This calibrates the player for a
  game that will not congratulate them.
- **The Figure is visible from four minutes away.** Silhouette it against the last light on the
  horizon the moment the player clears the fence. Everything after that is approach.
- **You never reach the Figure.** The Cut fires at ~6 m, mid-stride.

## 3. THE PUZZLE — *The Great Lens*

**Type:** optical alignment / perception
**Location:** the turret, beat 8
**Solve time:** 4–6 min

### Setup

The turret is a stone drum, half open to the sky. At its centre, on a rusted equatorial mount,
sits **the great lens** — a brass-collared refractor the size of a cannon, aimed down the valley
at the path. Beside it: a cold cup of tea on the parapet. Wind. The knocking, still audible, four
floors below.

Look through the lens and you get **nothing usable**. Two problems, in this order:

**(a) The glass is soiled.** Bird lime, streaked. The player drags a look-direction across the
eyepiece to wipe with a sleeve; the smear clears but leaves a greasy diagonal haze that never
fully goes. *You cannot clean it properly. You polish the glass with a dirty sleeve.* That is the
point — every subsequent view in the game keeps that faint diagonal smear in the corner.

**(b) The image is doubled.** Three concentric brass collars. Each rotates. The view through the
eyepiece shows the path as **two offset ghost images** — one cold petrol-blue, one warm ember —
drifting apart and together as the collars turn. This is a literal chromatic-misregistration
puzzle: the player is re-registering two print plates.

### Mechanic

- Collar **A** (outer) sets *distance* — pushes the two ghosts nearer or further along the path.
- Collar **B** (middle) sets *rotation* — twists them against each other.
- Collar **C** (inner) sets *convergence* — the fine overlap.
- Each collar **seats** when it comes close enough — it drops onto zero with a sound of brass
  finding its bed, and holds there. (See *Resolution doctrine* in the bible: the original "no
  click, no snap" rule made the solve a coin flip.) There is still no meter and no UI. The collar
  under your hands warms as it nears its seat; the rest is audio-visual: as the ghosts approach
  registration, the wind on the soundtrack thins and a low sub-bass swell rises. Perfect
  registration is a **silence**.

### Solve

When the two plates align, the doubled image resolves into a single sharp frame: **the orchard,
the ruin, and the Figure standing in it — with the player's own body standing six metres in front
of the Figure, seen from behind, mid-stride, frozen.**

Hold on that for four full seconds with no audio at all. Then the Watcher's hands tremble, the
collars slip, the image doubles again — and the CUT fires.

### Why this puzzle

The whole game is about two exposures of one person failing to line up. The first puzzle *is* the
thesis, performed with the player's hands, before a single word of it has been said. It also
teaches the game's grammar: **puzzles here are about seeing, not about mechanisms.** Nothing in
THE PATH is ever a keypad.

### Failure & assist

There is no fail state. If the player idles >90 s without touching a collar, the Watcher takes a
sip — *"Another sip."* — and the wind drops slightly, biasing the mix so the convergence tone is
easier to hear. Repeat every 90 s, up to three times, then let the assist plateau. Never solve it
for them.

**All VO is verbatim from PATH.pdf. Not one word is added, cut, or reordered.**

Delivery baseline: a dry male voice, close-mic'd, almost too close — you can hear the mouth.
No performance. He is not telling you a story; he is confirming things to himself. Pauses are
long. Never let a line land on a visual it describes — always **3–6 seconds late**, so the player
sees the thing, doubts it, and only then hears it named.

## 5. Visual Design

### Palette (locked — see 00_design_bible.md)

Chapter 1 uses the full range, because it is the only chapter that gets to. Everything after this
subtracts.

- **Moor black** `#0B1614` — mud, bark, water, the Figure
- **Petrol** `#16332F` — the dominant midtone; the air itself is this colour
- **Bruise cyan** `#3E6E70` — rain, wet stone, the far horizon
- **Bone** `#EDE2C2` — the path, and *only* the path, in this chapter
- **Ember** `#E0762A` — rim-light on the path verges, the lens brass, the one warm thing

No other hues exist. There is no green in the ivy, no red in the rot. Everything is graded into
these five. If the DP wants a sixth colour, the answer is no.

### Light

- **Single key**, extremely low and behind the horizon — permanent 8-minutes-past-sunset. Twilight
  *rides the path*: the light source is on the path's vanishing point, so walking forward is
  always walking into the light and everything you pass is a silhouette.
- **Rim-light discipline:** ember rims on every vertical edge that faces the vanishing point. This
  is what makes the cover image read, and it must be the chapter's signature.
- **No shadow from the player.** Not yet. The player casts no shadow for the whole game until
  Chapter 07, and nobody will consciously notice, and everybody will feel it.
- Interior turret: one practical, a guttering oil lamp, motivated ember. The mansion below is lit
  only by what leaks down the stair.

### Texture & post

The game should look like **a riso print that has been made to move.** Non-negotiable stack:

1. Render at a deliberately limited palette; posterise luminance to ~24 steps.
2. **Channel misregistration:** offset the cyan and ember separations by 1–2 px in a fixed
   direction, with a slow 0.2 Hz wander. This is the single most important effect in the game.
3. **Paper tooth:** a static 1:1 grain plate multiplied over the frame, *screen-locked, not
   world-locked* — it must sit on the glass in front of the world, like the smear on the lens.
4. Heavy edge vignette in **ash olive** `#2A2A22`, hard-ish, like a printed border.
5. Ink-bleed bloom: bloom only from bone and ember, never from petrol.
6. **No chromatic aberration, no lens flare, no film scratches.** Those are cinema. This is print.

### Signature frames to build the chapter around

- The bone path narrowing to a point between two ember verges, rain falling in sheets, one small
  figure. (The cover, exactly. Put it in the first thirty seconds so the player recognises where
  they are.)
- The dead orchard from inside a rank — black verticals, hard perspective, water reflecting a
  petrol sky.
- The Figure in the collapsed wall, ivy at its ankles, seen through the doorway like a portrait.
- The turret interior: the brass lens as a black silhouette against the last light, the cup on the
  parapet, wind taking the steam that isn't there because the tea is cold.

## 6. Audio Design

### Score

**Instrumentation for the whole game:** bowed double bass, prepared piano, a single detuned pump
organ, contact-mic'd stone and metal, and a female voice used only as texture (never as melody,
never with words). No drums, ever. No synth pads that sound like synth pads.

Chapter 1 uses **only the bass and the organ.** The organ is a drone, one chord, D minor with the
fifth slightly flat, present at –38 dB for the entire orchard sequence — beneath the rain, felt
rather than heard. It rises to –24 dB exactly when the Figure becomes visible and never resolves.

### Ambient

- **Rain is the bed** and it must be *layered by surface*: rain on water, rain on mud, rain on
  dead leaves, rain on stone, rain on the player's own head. Crossfade by proximity. This is where
  the chapter's budget goes.
- **Wind** is separate and is the Watcher's element — almost absent below, dominant in the turret.
  The two never appear at full strength together.
- **No wildlife.** No birds (though the birds have visibly been on the lens), no insects. The
  absence should be noticeable to anyone who has ever been outdoors.

### Foley

- **Bare feet.** This is the most important sound in the game. Mud suck, water, grit, and — in the
  orchard — the crack of rotted fruit underfoot. Record it for real, barefoot, in a wet field.
- The Watcher's stairs: dry, high, splintery squeal. Each step a distinct recorded asset, never
  randomised from a pool of three.
- The cup on stone: a small, clean, ceramic sound that is the only *pleasant* noise in the chapter.

### The Knocking — set up now, pay off in Ch. 03

Establish the knock **rhythm** here as a fixed, memorable pattern. Suggested:

```
knock ... knock-knock ......... knock
( 1 )    ( 2  3 )              ( 4 )
```

— a beat, two fast, a long gap, one. Roughly 4 s per cycle, never quantised, always the same
performance take. It plays behind beats 6 and 8. **Do not draw attention to it.** In Chapter 03
the player will have to reproduce it, and the design intent is that a first-time player half-knows
it without knowing why.

### Silence

Two engineered silences in this chapter, both absolute — no room tone, no floor:

1. Four seconds when the lens registers (§3).
2. Six seconds on black after *"I washed up on another shore."*

Silence is a resource. Chapter 1 spends it twice. Chapter 04 spends it once, for ninety seconds.

## 7. The Cut

The transition device for the entire game, established here. It is the book's `…` glyph made
physical.

**On every POV switch:**
1. One frame of pure **bone white** — full-screen, no fade.
2. A sound: an iris/shutter closing, pitched low, with a wet click in it. Recorded from an actual
   large-format camera shutter, slowed 30%.
3. The new POV is **already in motion** — never cut to a static frame. If the Watcher is mid-sip,
   you arrive mid-sip.
4. **The player's look-direction is preserved across the cut.** If you were looking left, you are
   looking left. This is what sells the two bodies as one nervous system.

Cuts are never player-triggered in Chapter 1. In Chapter 03 the player triggers one deliberately.
In Chapter 05 the player stops being able to tell which side they are on.

## 9. Exit

The coda card holds for eight seconds. Then the bone-white type dissolves and the title
**ASCENT** fades up in the same position, same face — implying it was always the next word on the
page.

Cut to black surf sound before the image arrives.
